What works, what doesn't

Bold lines

Dark marker or pen on white paper. The best case.

Solid shapes

Silhouettes and logos. The laser draws their outlines.

A few bright colors

Solid, saturated colors. Each line gets one color.

Photos & shading

Gradients have no clear lines, so they turn into noise.

Tiny detail

Hatching, dots and small text cause flicker or get lost.

Faint pencil

Light, sketchy lines get missed. Go over them in marker.

Designing for the JUMP wall

See it on the wall: click to play a video, click again to stop.

  • Draw tall: the wall canvas is about 2 wide by 3 tall. Use portrait paper and fill it top to bottom. Tall subjects (people, animals standing up, towers, plants) look great.
  • Stay off the edges: leave a little space around your drawing. Lines that reach the sides spill onto the glass and get lost.
  • Bold and simple: people watch from across the plaza and the street. Big shapes and a few lines read best from far away.
  • Two or three colors: red, blue, green and pink all show up well on the dark wall.
  • Check the preview: the dashed box on the laser preview is the wall canvas. Everything should stay inside it.
Limits at a glance
  • Files: JPG or PNG, any size. Number them in order (run_01.jpg, run_02.jpg …).
  • Keep frames lined up: same canvas size and the drawing in the same place, or the animation jumps around.
  • Detail: about 1,000 laser points per frame, roughly 10 to 30 pen strokes. More than that flickers and looks dimmer.
  • Separate pieces: keep it under about 50 per frame. Every gap between pieces costs time with the beam off.
  • Colors: the laser mixes red, green and blue light, so use up to about 6 bright, solid colors. Black can't be drawn (it's "off"), and gray or black lines show as white.
  • Line weight: about 1/100 of the picture's width or thicker, and even throughout.
  • Text: big, bold, short. A word or two, not sentences.
  • Frames: no hard limit; 8 to 24 is typical. Playback speed is set in LaserOS when you import.

How to make a laser animation

  1. Draw your frames. Dark marker on white paper works best. Draw tall, keep each drawing in the same spot, and leave space around the edges. No paper? instead, then skip to step 4. You can also draw on top of uploaded frames.
  2. Photograph or scan them and number the files in order: dog_01.jpg, dog_02.jpg …
  3. Open "1 Draw or upload frames" on the left and drop your files in. Want to practice first? .
  4. Check the laser preview. Press Play. Everything should stay inside the dashed wall canvas box. If the lines look messy, try "2 Tracing & simplify". To make it bigger, smaller or move it, use "5 Scale & position".
  5. Pick a color in "3 Color", or use the colors from your drawing.
  6. Looks choppy? Try "7 Smooth motion" to add in-between frames.
  7. Open "8 Export", give it a name and click Download .ild.
  8. In LaserOS, import the .ild file and choose how fast it plays.

You can skip the other settings. The defaults are tuned for the JUMP wall.
